## What happened

`change-approval.sh --delegate`, and the check over a delegated line, now refuse the stops
`/propose` step 3a kept as prose: a change touching `ADR_DIR` or `PRACTICES_DIR`; a proposal with
any `**Approval:**` line; a roadmap row saying `Approval: owner` (any case, outside backticks); a
`**Risk:**` below the row's (a row with none is normal); and a proposal whose history, on the branch
or `origin/main` and across a rename of its directory, ever held an Approved line. The row is read
from `origin/main` through `roadmap_queue`, as the one row whose change cell names the change's id,
and the proposal's `**Roadmap row:**` must be it. Step 3a shrinks to the judgment the script cannot
make. PANEL-56, PANEL-57, OPS-42, OPS-92a, OPS-112, OPS-113, OPS-116 and OPS-117 carry
`(Approval: owner)` in their change cells. Every new refusal has a negative case confirmed failing
on a mutant of its line first.

## Decisions

- The row is found by the change id in `origin/main`'s roadmap, not by the proposal's `**Roadmap
  row:**`: round 2 showed a proposal could name a laxer row and borrow its Risk. No row, two rows,
  or a mismatch refuses.
- Everything fails closed: an unreadable roadmap, an unparseable Risk, two Risks in one row, an
  unreadable history. A refusal goes to the owner, who can always `--record`.
- A reused change id inherits its predecessor's history, so the refusal names it and a new change
  takes a new id: cheaper than separating lineages, and ids are free.
- Round 3's two lows (a branch-only revoke is lost if the branch is rewritten; `--follow` can read
  another change's history when one commit swaps them) are deferred to #272, not fixed past the
  cap: both fail toward the owner or toward a branch Claude would have to rewrite on purpose, and
  the README now says where the guarantee is firm.

## Surprises

  the owner had approved by hand the day before. Rule 4 again: the stop had lived in prose that
  each proposal's author re-read.
